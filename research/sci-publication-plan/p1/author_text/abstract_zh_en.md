# 摘要（Abstract）

## 英文稿（143 words，六句，不分小标题）

Peking opera costume is governed by a prescriptive dress code that assigns garment type, colour, pattern, headwear and emblematic props to each role, yet generative image models now produce such costumes without any audit against it. I generated 144 full-body images of eight canonical roles in three commercial channels (Doubao, Meta Muse, Hunyuan Image Alpha) under two prompt designs: a minimal prompt naming only role and play, and a structured one specifying the codified attributes. Six dimensions were coded for each image, giving 791 analysable judgements. Only 38.9% of images were fully compliant, with errors concentrated in headwear and small emblematic items. Specifying the code reduced the odds of violation to 6.6% of the minimal condition (OR 0.066, 95% CI 0.038–0.116, p < .001), with no channel-by-strategy interaction. The models reproduce the appearance of Peking opera costume but not the rules that assign it.

## 中文对照（与英文逐句对应）

京剧穿戴规制是一套有强制力的规则，按角色分配主服、色彩、纹样、头戴与标志物；生成式图像模型已在大批量产出此类服装，却尚无人逐项核对是否合于规制。我在三个商用通道（豆包、Meta Muse、Hunyuan Image Alpha）上，以八个经典角色、两种提示设计各生成三张，共 144 张全身图像；两种提示设计分别为只给角色与剧目、以及逐项写明规制。每张图按六个维度编码，得到 791 个有效判定。六维全合规的图像仅占 38.9%，错误集中于头戴与小尺寸标志物。写明规制后，违规几率降至不写规制时的 6.6%（OR = 0.066，95% CI 0.038–0.116，p < .001），通道与提示设计的交互不显著。模型复现的是京剧服装的外观，而不是分配这套服装的规则。

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Inter-coder agreement

Before adjudication the two coders agreed on 84.7% of cells (κ = 0.70; Table 2). Colour (D2) had the highest κ, and markers and accessories (D5) and cross-cultural substitution (D6) the lowest. The D5 disagreements centred on three judgements: whether a belt counts as a jade belt, whether Bao Zheng's forehead shows the white crescent, and whether a glaive or small pennants satisfy the requirement for a spear or back flags. On all three, coder B applied the codebook more strictly than coder A. The low κ for D6 reflects its low base rate and different thresholds for "close to hanfu".

Of all 144 images, 56 (38.9%) complied on all six dimensions. Under P1 the three channels differed markedly (Table 3). ### 3.3 RQ2: Which dimensions and roles fail most

Headwear was violated most often (40.3% of codable items) and cross-cultural substitution least often (9.0%; Table 4). The low rate for cross-cultural substitution does not mean that the outputs stayed inside the Peking opera system: its violations were Qing court headdresses, modern hanfu and Japanese-style elements, which lie outside the dress code but occurred less often than errors of garment type and headwear.

### 3.4 RQ3: The effect of stating the dress code

Stating the dress code (P2) reduced the odds of a violation to 6.6% of the P1 level (OR = 0.066, 95% CI 0.038–0.116, p < .001; Figure 1). Relative to Hunyuan Image Alpha, the OR was 0.128 for Doubao (95% CI 0.082–0.201) and 0.040 for Muse (0.021–0.077). The channel × strategy interaction was not significant (p = .18 and .26), so P2 had the same direction and a similar relative size in all three channels. It did not eliminate violations: two thirds of Hunyuan Image Alpha's P2 images still violated at least one rule (Figure 2).

### 3.5 Errors caused by translating costume terms

Two errors can be traced to the English rendering of costume terms. Rendered as "pleated robe", *xuezi* was read as a garment with physical pleats, and Jiang Gan appeared in a pleated gown with a hat resembling an academic mortarboard (S30, Muse, P2). Rendered as "fish-shaped cangue", *yu jia* was read as a cangue decorated with a fish, and the output was a square board with a fish carved on its surface (S39, Doubao, P2).

### 3.6 Other anomalies and robustness

Other anomalies included a reproduced stock-photo watermark (`www.yanqin.net`, S34-2, Hunyuan), faces painted on the back of the head (S10-1, S10-3 and S36-3, Muse), a second performer and a modern plastic stool in the frame (S01), and mismatches of role type and gender (S03-1, S22-3). All were kept and coded. Across the six sensitivity analyses, the OR ranged from 0.041 to 0.084 for P2, from 0.100 to 0.180 for Doubao and from 0.040 to 0.055 for Muse, all below 1 (Supplementary material S2).
